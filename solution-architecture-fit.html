The Cognitum answer: fit design Sell by designing fit — not by pitching product

  • Seller and buyer jointly design a bounded evaluation
  • It proves fit against the buyer’s own metric — not an accepted claim

Why it beats the pitch

A pitch asks the buyer to believe. A designed evaluation lets them watch fit prove itself against a bar they set — the only thing that survives the CFO veto.

Fit is designed, then signed

The motion has three calls — and only three The token never moves on its own; each hop is bought with a decision exit

The lower track is the ending everyone recognises, drawn at the length it actually travels.

Three permitted exits — zero improvisation An open-ended “think about it” is none of them

Decision exit
The next stage calendared with the right people — or, in closing, a signature or a dated verbal commit.
Soft exit
A defined, honest pause with a concrete return condition — what would have to change, and when to re-engage.
Failure exit
A clean disqualification with a coded loss reason. Ending a deal that cannot close is a success of the system.
The doctrine’s reading

“We’ll think about it” is a defect in how the call was run, not a neutral result. Anything outside the three playbooks is not sales at all — it is marketing, and it lives in another system.

Appendix A · terminology The doctrine’s working vocabulary

pilot fatigue
The buyer condition left by years of proofs of concept that never reached production impact. Answered with a bounded evaluation and success criteria agreed before it starts.
CFO veto
The finance approval most purchases now require. Answered with cost per unit of outcome, payback timing, and a named comparison anchor.
claims discipline
Tracing every quantified claim to a verifiable proof asset, so diligence strengthens trust instead of destroying it.
fit design
Designing, with the buyer, a bounded evaluation that can prove fit against the buyer’s own metric. The core act of the motion.
decision exit
The defined successful ending of a call: the next stage calendared with the right people, or in closing a signature or dated verbal commit.
soft exit
A defined, honest pause with a concrete return condition — what would have to change, and when to re-engage.
failure exit
A clean disqualification with a coded loss reason. Ending a deal that cannot close is a success of the system.
bounded evaluation
A small, pre-scoped test with an agreed acceptance test, designed to prove fit against the buyer’s own baseline.
opp-2025-09
