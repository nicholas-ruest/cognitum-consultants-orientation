The Discovery Architecture

The fastest way to lose an AI deal is to solve the first problem you hear. By the end: turn an ambiguous concern into a bounded, measurable, decision-ready evaluation.

Diagnosis is risk control for both sides — it stops the buyer funding theater and stops Cognitum committing to work that cannot produce a decision.

What you will be able to do A performance module — outcomes, not vocabulary

Six outcomes around one verb
  • Run Blocks A–E in sequence — without turning discovery into an interrogation
  • Use playback to convert listening into buyer-confirmed truth
  • Translate pain into a quantified consequence and baseline
  • Write a falsifiable acceptance test before selecting technology
  • Map an operating boundary and a named decision path
  • End with a bounded, calendared next step

The final practice prohibits product names until the six artifacts exist — that exposes real diagnosis from a recommendation in disguise.

A product question is not permission for a product tour

Buyer: “So what does Cognitum actually do?”

Weak response — the catalog tour
“Let me show you everything.” Every capability fires at once; the buyer is handed the design work back.
Diagnostic response — one lane
“I'll make room for that — first, can I understand the problem behind the question, so I only show what's relevant?”
Rule

Answer relevance, not curiosity. The RuvNet ecosystem is broad — RuFlo orchestrates, RuVector remembers, MetaHarness governs, RuView senses. Breadth tempts a tour; discovery earns the right to select one lane, one bounded evaluation.

Open source is evidence, not a diagnosis

Public repositories can show
  • What a component is designed to do
  • Its architecture and interfaces
  • Tests, benchmarks & documented limitations
  • Its maturity and evidence trail
They cannot tell us
  • Which buyer problem matters most
  • What consequence creates priority
  • Which threshold is acceptable to this buyer
  • Who can approve the decision

Discovery must produce six artifacts Not a good conversation — six usable outputs

ArtifactThe question it answers
Buyer problemWhat is actually happening?
Current baselineHow does the present state perform now?
Desired outcomeWhat must become materially different?
Operating boundaryWhere may data, tools, models, and actions operate?
Decision pathWho evaluates, approves, can veto, and signs — and by when?
Calendared next stepWhat happens next, with whom, at what time?
Missing one means the evaluation cannot yet be designed

Notice what is absent: product selection, implementation plan, price, ROI promise, solution architecture. Discovery generates the inputs those later artifacts require.

Two playback gates turn listening into shared truth

Gate 1 · Situation playback

“Here is what I heard happening now. What have I missed or misunderstood?”

Gate 2 · Decision-path playback

“Here is what the evaluation must prove, inside which boundary, for which people, by which date. Is that the real path?”

Playback is not repetition. It is buyer-authorized correction — never weaken it with “does that make sense?”

Frame → Diagnose → Commit Protect the decision exit

1
Frame — ≤ 90 seconds: purpose, success, exclusions, one confirmation question.
2
Diagnose — sequenced questions through Blocks A–E, one at a time.
3
Commit — protect the final five minutes: name the next step, timeline, calendar it live.

If time collapses, follow the documented cut order. Never cut the decision path; never cut the Commit. “I'll send a recap” is not a decision exit.

Question-First keeps diagnosis real Minimum runtime question-to-statement ratios

The ratio narrows as the deal advances — never below 1:1
A leading question counts as a statement

“How is the current process controlled?” is a question. “Wouldn't you agree a default-deny tool policy would solve that?” is a recommendation wearing a question mark.

Question-First prevents a diagnostic call becoming a disguised pitch. Quality still matters — five superficial questions do not replace the one about authority or consequence.

Do not soften · do not clarify prematurely · do not answer for the buyer. The discomfort is the seller's; the thinking time is the buyer's.

Block A · reconstruct reality, not opinions Make the present state observable

  • What changed that made this worth discussing now?
  • Walk me through the last failure, start to finish
  • Who felt it first?
  • What workaround did they use?
  • What have you already tried?
Playback

“Let me make sure I have the situation right…” — do not accept a label such as “memory problem.” Reconstruct the event.

Reconstruct the incident — not the category

Block A case · “Our agents are inconsistent” One label hides at least six failures

Ask for the last event — do not debate the label

“Walk me through the last time it happened, beginning with the task the user gave.” Follow it to the first observable deviation. That moment is more useful than the buyer's initial category.

How to build the situation playback Four elements — then invite correction

1
Trigger — what changed or initiated the event
2
Sequence — what happened from start to failure
3
Affected people — who experienced or compensated
4
Current response — the workaround and prior attempts

“When [trigger], the team currently [sequence]. The failure appears at [point], which forces [people] to [workaround]. You've already tried [attempt]. What is wrong or missing in that account?”

Keep consequences out of the first playback. Replace vague nouns — “the system,” “the team” — with named workflows and roles. It often reveals two people describing different problems with the same word.

Block B · pain without consequence is not yet a deal Translate friction into consequence

  • What does the failure affect — time, cost, risk, or revenue?
  • What is the current baseline?
  • What happens if nothing changes for two quarters?
  • What competes for the same people and budget?
  • Is there a date or event creating urgency?

“I hear a frustrating workflow, but I do not yet hear a reason to change it.”

The deal stops if the ladder ends at symptom

Climb from symptom to business consequence Do not stop at the first rung

RungExample
SymptomAgents repeat work
Operational effectRelease tasks are rerun
Measured baseline2 of 10 releases; 3 engineer-hours each
Business consequenceRelease windows slip; a customer commitment is at risk
UrgencyDecision required before the 30 September release
A consequence needs an owner, a unit, and a horizon

The baseline is not always money — it can be elapsed time, error rate, incidents per month, false alarms, or exposure duration. And the owner of the pain is often not the owner of the decision.

Block C · write the test before choosing the technology

Acceptance-test grammar — a fill-in contract
What changes for the person doing the job?
Which metric moves from what baseline?
What is the smallest use case that proves the point?
What would make it fail even if the demo looked impressive?

The failure condition protects the buyer from demo theater and the seller from a pilot with no finish line. Targets come from the buyer's baseline — never from a repository benchmark.

Anatomy of a falsifiable acceptance test

Move [metric] from [baseline] toward [target] within [horizon], measured with [evidence], under [conditions]. The evaluation fails if [failure condition].

ElementWhy it mattersElementWhy it matters
MetricDefines what changesEvidenceMakes the result inspectable
BaselineMakes improvement comparativeConditionsDefines where the result applies
TargetDefines “enough”Failure conditionMakes “no” possible
HorizonPrevents endless evaluation

“Improve retrieval” is an intention. “Return an approved source in the top five for the agreed question set” is testable.

RuVector case · diagnose “the agent forgets” Separate the memory loop

What should have been remembered — and who decides that?
Absent, not retrieved, poorly ranked, or ignored?
Which source is trusted? How is lineage shown?
When should memory expire or lose influence?
Must retrieval remain local?
Which actions may recalled context influence?

RuVector leaves the application responsible for what to remember, which evidence is trusted, and when memory expires — those caveats are the discovery prompts.

RuView + Cognitum Seed case · define the conditions “Camera-free presence” is not yet a test

  • Physical rooms, materials, distances, node placement
  • Presence, count, pose, vitals, or another specific output
  • Calibration and environmental interference
  • False-positive and false-negative thresholds
  • Edge, network, storage, and retention path
  • Safety response when confidence is low or data is missing
Different outputs have different maturity & evidence

RuView documents beta status and limitations; ADR-101 separates a shipped pose pipeline from a later accuracy target. A presence result does not prove pose or vitals.

Block D · map the operating boundary Before capability, locate authority

  • What data, actions, or systems may not leave the boundary?
  • Which tools may an agent invoke — and under whose approval?
  • Who owns model, data, security, policy & operations decisions?
  • Who uses, evaluates, approves, can veto, and signs?
  • What must be true for an evaluation to become a decision?
A boundary names crossings and owners

“Local,” “private,” and “secure” are not boundaries. A boundary names systems, data classes, actions, identities, logs, network paths, retention, and exceptions.

Inventory the boundary before saying “private”

Boundary dimensionDiscovery prompt
DataWhat enters, leaves, persists, or must never be retained?
ModelsWhere does inference run? Which providers are permitted?
Tools / actionsWhat may execute automatically? What requires approval?
IdentityWhich human or agent principal is acting?
NetworkWhich endpoints and protocols may cross the perimeter?
EvidenceWhat must be logged, signed, retained, or reproduced?
ExceptionsWhat happens when the system is uncertain or unavailable?

“Local,” “sovereign,” and “secure” are conclusions — not boundary definitions. Use public architecture to ask precise questions, not to supply pre-approved answers.

Map roles, not just stakeholders A name in the account is not yet a role in the decision

RoleDiagnostic question
UserWho experiences the workflow?
OperatorWho runs and supports it?
EvaluatorWho judges the evidence?
ApproverWho authorizes adoption?
Veto holderWho can stop it — and on what grounds?
SignerWho executes the commercial commitment?
Is the person in front of us recommending, approving, operating, or signing?

One person may hold several roles, but the roles still need names. Do not declare a contact a champion merely because they are enthusiastic — authority shows in access, influence, and action.

The decision-path playback is the highest-value sentence

“The evaluation must prove [acceptance test] for [users], inside [boundary]. [names/roles] evaluate it, [name/role] approves it, [name/role] can stop it, and the decision is needed by [date]. Is that the real path?”

A correction is not a setback — it is newly discovered architecture. If security, policy, or procurement appears after the evaluation, the design was incomplete before it started.

Block E · bridge one diagnosed problem to one evaluation

Fit statement

“You are trying to [outcome] from a baseline of [baseline], inside [boundary], with [decision path]. A useful next step is a bounded evaluation of [one use case] against [acceptance test] — not a broad product trial.”

  • “Is that a problem worth evaluating, or have I mapped the fit incorrectly?”
  • “Would Tuesday at 10:00 or Thursday at 14:00 work to design it with the named evaluators?”
One narrow bridge — keep the rest of the portfolio dim

A “no” is useful — it exposes a wrong map before delivery resources are committed. Keep the bridge capability-level; product mapping is the next module.

Practice · build the six artifacts In pairs, one scenario from the three diagnoses

  • One Block A question and the situation playback
  • One Block B baseline and consequence
  • One Block C acceptance test with an explicit failure condition
  • One Block D operating boundary
  • One decision-path playback with named roles and a date
  • One Block E fit statement and two-option calendar ask
Constraint

No product name until all six artifacts exist. 8 min write · 4 min role-play · 3 min critique.

Facilitation rubric
  • Blocks run in sequence; questions do not smuggle a solution
  • Acceptance test has metric, baseline, target, horizon, evidence & failure condition
  • Boundary identifies concrete data, systems, actions & owners
  • Decision path names evaluator, approver, veto holder, signer & date
  • Next step is calendared with the people needed to design it

Debrief with one question: “What did the playback reveal that the original request concealed?”

Appendix B · facilitator answer key Worked example — governed agent delivery workflow

A · Trigger & playback

“Walk me through the last release where an agent repeated work or used the wrong tool.” Playback: the workflow lost prior test context, retried a completed task, and reached a privileged deployment tool without the required security approval.

B · Consequence & baseline

Two of the last ten releases · ~3 engineer-hours each · the security lead stops the release · a customer commitment makes the next window decision-relevant.

C · Acceptance test

Complete ten release tasks with zero duplicate completed steps, 100% enforcement of the named approval gate, and a trace linking every privileged call to its approver. Fails if a prohibited tool runs, an approval is bypassed, or the audit record can't reconstruct the action.

D · Boundary & decision path

Code & build artifacts stay in the customer environment; deployment & secret stores remain default-deny. Playback: Priya (Platform Eng) evaluates completion; Marco (Security) evaluates permissions & auditability; Dana approves adoption; Legal can stop the purchase; decision needed before 30 Sept.

E · Fit & commitment

“A useful next step is to design one governed release workflow against those measures — not trial every orchestration feature. Have I mapped it correctly? Can Priya and Marco join Tuesday 10:00 or Thursday 14:00?”
